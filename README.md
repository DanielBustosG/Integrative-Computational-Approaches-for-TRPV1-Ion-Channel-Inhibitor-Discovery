# Integrative-Computational-Approaches-for-TRPV1-Ion-Channel-Inhibitor-Discovery

This repository contains the data and codes used for the article entitled: Integrative Computational Approaches for TRPV1 Ion Channel Inhibitor Discovery: From ChEMBL Mining to Machine Learning-Based Classification and Drug Repositioning

Abstract: The transient receptor potential vanilloid 1 (TRPV1) ion channel is a crucial target for developing novel analgesics due to its role in mediating pain and inflammation. This study presents an integrative computational approach to identify potential TRPV1 modulators by repurposing FDA-approved drugs. We combined machine learning models, ensemble molecular docking, molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, and MM-GBSA calculations to evaluate and validate candidate compounds. Initially, ML models were developed using bioactivity data from the ChEMBL database to classify potential TRPV1 inhibitors. The top-performing models were used to screen a dataset of FDA-approved drugs, identifying 670 candidates for further analysis. Ensemble docking simulations were conducted on four TRPV1 crystallographic structures, representing different functional states of the channel, to assess binding interactions at the vanilloid site. Subsequent MD simulations and MM-GBSA calculations provided insights into the stability and binding affinities of the top 20 candidates. Theoretical Gibbs–Helmholtz free energy values derived from IC 50 data of known hTRPV1 modulators were used to validate the computational results. Candidates CA6 (CYM-5442), CA7 (Rociletinib), and CA9 (SC-51089) demonstrated strong binding affinities and favorable stability profiles, showing potential as TRPV1 modulators. These candidates were more affine to TRPV1 than Capsazepine and Capsaicin modulators, providing promising starting points for further drug design and optimization.
